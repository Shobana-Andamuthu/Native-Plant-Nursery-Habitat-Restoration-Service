Purple Coneflower

Echinacea purpurea · Asteraceae (Daisy Family)

Ecological Interaction

Faunal Association: Visited by over 40 species of native bees, including long-horned bees (Melissodes), leafcutter bees, and bumblebees.

Winter Wildlife Value: Stiff seed cones persist above snow, offering high-fat sustenance for American Goldfinches, chickadees, and nuthatches.

Purple Coneflower

Echinacea purpurea (L.) Moench
$8.50 / 1-Quart Pot · $3.25 / Deep-Cell Plug (Tray of 50)

An iconic perennial of Midwestern prairies and open woods, Purple Coneflower is one of the most reliable and ecologically rewarding plants in North America. It establishes deep fibrous taproots, resists prolonged midsummer drought, and produces vibrant daisy-like blossoms with drooping rose-purple ray florets around a spiny orange-bronze cone.

Native Range Eastern & Central North America (USDA Zones 3–9)
Sunlight Requirements Full Sun (6+ hours) to Light Dappled Shade
Soil & Moisture Preference Dry to Medium; Well-drained loam, clay, or rocky soils
Mature Plant Dimensions 2.5 to 4 feet Height · 1.5 to 2 feet Spread
Blooming Season June through August (up to 8 weeks sustained bloom)
Deer & Drought Resistance High drought tolerance; Moderate deer browse resistance
Planting Density 12 to 18 inches on center for meadow establishment
