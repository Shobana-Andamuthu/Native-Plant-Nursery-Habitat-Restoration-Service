Regional Native Planting Guide

Field-tested botanical advice on eco-region selection, soil biology, hydration gradients, and calendar timing for self-sustaining native plantings.

EPA Level III Eco-regions
Provenance Matching

Selecting Plants by Eco-Region, Not State Lines

Plants don't recognize political boundaries; they adapt to geological bedrock, frost dates, and annual rainfall patterns. When choosing native species, always reference your local EPA Eco-region to verify genetic suitability.

True Local Ecotypes vs. Cultivars ("Nativars")

Unlike hybridized ornamental cultivars bred for flower color or dwarf size, wild open-pollinated ecotypes maintain rich nectar chemistry and evolutionary insect cues.

Layered Plant Communities

Successful native plantings operate in vertical layers: structural canopy, understory matrix, architectural perennials, and dense ground-layer "green mulch" sedges that block weeds.

Diagnostic Factors

Evaluating Your Site Conditions

Analyze your physical site factors before purchasing plants. Right plant, right place ensures 100% success.

Sunlight Exposure

Full Sun: 6+ hours of uninterrupted light (Prairie forbs & warm-season grasses).
Part Sun: 3–6 hours of filtered light (Woodland edges & dappled understory).
Full Shade: Filtered canopy with indirect light (Spring ephemerals & sedges).

Soil Texture & pH

Clay Soil: High nutrient density with slower drainage (Coneflowers & Asters).
Sandy Soil: Rapid percolation with lean fertility (Butterfly Weed & Bluestem).
Loam Soil: Balanced living humus supporting the broadest regional flora diversity.

Moisture Gradients

Xeric (Dry): Upland ridges and sandy slopes with minimal standing moisture.
Mesic (Med): Retains moisture 2 days after rainfall without chronic waterlogging.
Hydric (Wet): Standing water or saturated wetland soil (Rain gardens & basins).
Annual Timeline

Seasonal Planting & Care Protocol

Exact seasonal windows for plug installation, cold-stratified dormant seeding, and weed suppression.

Spring (April–June)

Plug Installation & Rapid Rooting

Ideal window for planting deep-cell native root plugs. Root crowns establish deep downward anchor channels before shoot expansion. Irrigate once weekly during month one if rainfall remains sparse.

  • Apply shredded leaf mulch to bare soil
  • Avoid synthetic chemical fertilizers
Summer (July–August)

Weed Management & Bloom Support

Perform timed high-mowing above eight inches across establishing meadows. Suppresses annual weed seedheads while allowing lower native rosettes to absorb sunlight and expand perennial biomass.

  • Selectively hand-pull taproot weeds
  • Leave mature seedheads for wild birds
Autumn & Winter (Oct–Dec)

Dormant Seeding & Overwintering

Optimal window for dormant broadcast seeding across prepared soil. Natural freeze-thaw cycles draw seeds into the topsoil, breaking embryo dormancy for synchronous early spring germination.

  • Broadcast dormant seed onto snow
  • Leave upright stalks for solitary bees
